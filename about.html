Author & Editorial Background

Bridging Jobsite Mud with Architectural Physics

"Building science shouldn't read like an unfeeling laboratory report. If you've ever stood in wool socks on a freezing January morning listening to wind whistle through baseboard gaps, you already understand thermodynamics—you just need the language to fix it."

Raised in Construction, Formed by Architecture

I grew up inside a Midwestern construction family. Long before I ever opened an architectural textbook, my weekends were spent on jobsites—watching framing crews stand 2x6 walls, seeing concrete pumpers pour footings in freezing clay, and feeling the physical realities of weather on unconditioned wood.

I went on to earn a professional, NAAB-accredited Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch), where I immersed myself in structural assemblies, building envelope thermodynamics, environmental systems, and material science. But that dual background left me with an abiding appreciation for the tension between the drawing board and the real world: theoretical details often fail if they ignore how a carpenter swings a hammer or how a mason lays brick in the cold.

MO

Grounded in Missouri: Heart of Climate Zone 1 Very Hot-Humid & Island Climates

Based in Missouri, I live in one of the most demanding environmental transition zones on the continent. Within any twelve-month period, our homes must endure sub-zero polar vortexes driving heat outward at -15°F, followed immediately by sweltering 95°F summer heatwaves with 80% relative humidity forcing subterranean moisture inward. A home built for one extreme will rot under the other unless its envelope is intelligently tuned to breathe and dry.

Lived Experience: From Drafty Cavities to Tight Envelopes

My perspective isn't derived solely from CAD software or sensor telemetry. I've lived in the full spectrum of Midwestern architecture:

  • •
    The Historic Pre-War Home: Battling balloon-framed 24-foot exterior stud chimneys, uninsulated stone basements, and pocketed floor joists in solid brick wythes where you can physically feel the stack effect siphoning warm air into the attic.
  • •
    The Mid-Century Ranch: Wrestling with shallow roof trusses, unvented soffits, massive thermal bridging across 2x4 studs, and devastating roof ice dams whenever snow falls on an unsealed ceiling plane.
  • •
    Modern High-Performance Builds: Experiencing the quiet comfort, balanced ventilation (ERVs), and low utility footprint of continuous exterior stone wool, air-sealed drywall assemblies, and variable-speed inverter heat pumps.

Why Narrative Writing Belongs in Building Science

Most technical publications make a fatal mistake: they reduce building science to dense equations and dry ASTM citations that alienate the very people who own and renovate these homes.

I love narrative writing. In every article on Tropical AEC, I aim to weave real spatial storytelling, jobsite grit, and human observations together with rigorous engineering physics. When you understand why a joist rots or how pressure differentials pull cold air through a mud sill, you make smarter renovation choices, spend capital where it actually delivers ROI, and avoid predatory sales pitches for high-margin gimmicks.
