## 1. Geographic Scope & Thermodynamic Climate Profile

- **Target Geographic Regions (Island & Deep Tropical):** Hawaii (Oahu, Maui, Big Island, Kauai), Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands (St. Thomas, St. Croix, St. John), Guam, South Florida / Florida Keys.
- **IECC / ASHRAE 90.1 Climate Zones:**
  - **Zone 1A (Very Hot - Humid):** Year-round dew points continuously between 72°F and 78°F. Average ambient temperatures ranging from 78°F to 92°F with virtually zero seasonal heating degree days (HDD = 0). Continuous inward vapor pressure 365 days a year with zero winter drying cycles.
  - **Island Marine Microclimates:** Windward coastal plains facing relentless trade winds and maritime sea-spray salt aerosol; leeward arid rain-shadow slopes; high-altitude volcanic rainforest microclimates (200+ inches annual rainfall).
- **Core Building Physics & Structural Challenges:**
  - **Extreme Atmospheric Salt-Spray Galvanic Corrosion:** Airborne marine chloride ions attacking structural fasteners, electrical service panels, metal roofing clips, and aluminum window frames within 1 to 3 miles of the coastline; requirement for Type 316 marine-grade stainless steel fasteners and hot-dip galvanized G90 / ZAM coatings.
  - **Cyclonic Super-Typhoon & Category 5 Wind Loads:** Island building envelopes subject to ASCE 7 basic wind speeds of 150 to 195 MPH. Complete structural load paths required: cast-in-place concrete tie beams, reinforced concrete masonry units (CMU), heavy-gauge cyclonic hurricane clips, and impact-resistant louvered fenestrations.
  - **High Electricity Costs & Grid Fragility (Island Solar + Battery Islanding):** Island power costs ranging from $0.35 to $0.55 per kWh (Hawaii, PR, USVI) with frequent hurricane blackout vulnerabilities; intense demand for off-grid resilient solar PV, lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) battery storage, and passive cooling envelopes.
  - **Torrential Tropical Rain Squalls & Wind-Driven Water Entry:** Intense horizontal rainfall rates (4 to 6 inches per hour) during tropical depressions driving water past standard window weepholes, soffit vents, and low-slope roof laps.
  - **Formosan Termite Decimation & Rot:** Continuous high ambient humidity and lack of freezing temperatures allowing subterranean and drywood termites to consume untreated wood framing within 2 to 4 years; strong mandate for light-gauge steel framing, autoclaved aerated concrete (AAC), or borate-treated structural lumber.
  - **Open-Air Passive Ventilation vs Air-Conditioned Hygrothermal Conflicts:** Hybrid buildings operating with open louvers during day/evening, then switching to closed air-conditioning at night; massive interstitial condensation risk inside walls when cool interior surfaces meet 80°F / 85% RH air.

## 2. Dominant Regional Architectural Vernacular & Housing Archetypes

1. **Hawaiian Plantation Style (1900–1940):** Single-wall construction (vertical 1x6 tongue-and-groove fir without wall studs), wide board-and-batten eaves, hip roofs with corrugated iron cladding, raised on post-and-pier concrete footings for natural underfloor convective cooling.
2. **Puerto Rican & Caribbean Cast Concrete Monolithic (1950–Present):** Reinforced cast-in-place concrete flat roof slabs and exterior concrete walls, jalousie louvered windows, designed to resist hurricane wind shear and withstand termite attacks.
3. **South Florida Stucco Concrete Block & Tile (1960–Present):** Monolithic slab-on-grade, reinforced CMU exterior walls, concrete roof tile or metal roofing, impact-rated sliding glass doors opening to covered lanais.
4. **Modern Tropical Sustainable Vernacular (2000–Present):** Deep overhanging eaves (4 to 6 feet) shielding solar heat from glass, clerestory louvers inducing convective stack-effect ventilation, light-gauge structural steel frames, standing-seam aluminum or Galvalume roofs with high solar reflectance index (SRI > 80).
5. **Island Stilt / Pier Pole Homes (Coastal & Hillside):** Driven timber or steel pilings elevating the living floor above coastal flood surges (FEMA V-Zones) and steep volcanic slopes, exposed to high under-floor wind uplift pressures.
