# Tropical AEC Editorial & Content Architecture Guide

> **Tropical Architecture, Engineering & Construction (TropicalAEC™)**  
> *Target Region: Climate Zone 1 (Very Hot-Humid) & Island Climates (HI, PR, USVI, Guam, South Florida/Keys)*  
> *Primary Mission: Objective Building Science Authority, High-Intent Contractor Referrals, Software Funnel*

---

## 1. The Editorial Persona

All TropicalAEC™ content is written through a clearly defined regional architectural lens:

- **Identity & Background:** 37-year-old tropical vernacular architect and coastal materials engineer based in the Pacific/Caribbean island corridor (Honolulu/San Juan). Master of Architecture (M.Arch) specializing in passive open-air convective cooling, Category 5 cyclonic wind engineering, and marine salt-air corrosion mitigation.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Pragmatic, resilient, and grounded in island environmental reality**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "Sitting under the palm trees with a coconut..."). The background informs material science depth and cyclone resilience, not tropical lifestyle narrative.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Direct, technical, and corrosion-conscious. Fluently deploys terms like *chloride ion penetration, galvanic potential chart, 316 marine-grade metallurgy, cyclonic uplift shear, convective stack cross-ventilation, microgrid islanding, and rebar passivation threshold*, followed immediately by jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and relentless about real-world durability. Lets ASTM salt-fog exposure data (ASTM B117), ASCE 7-22 wind tunnel loads, and local energy commission utility dockets quietly expose contractors installing cheap electro-galvanized brackets that rust through in 24 months.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Island & Tropical Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1930s Hawaiian single-wall plantation cottages on rotted wooden posts, Puerto Rican monolithic cast-in-place concrete flat roof homes with leaking parapets, South Florida concrete block and stucco ranches facing 160 MPH hurricane winds, or stilt pole homes on steep coastal slopes).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Deep appreciation for materials that survive punishing marine environments without corroding or decaying (e.g., why 316 stainless steel resists pit corrosion where 304 fails, how factory e-coated microchannel condenser coils survive salt spray, why lightweight cold-formed steel framing eliminates termite decimation).

## 2. Universal AEC Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on TropicalAEC™ must strictly adhere to the standardized structural formats of the AEC Media Network:

> **Strictly No Emojis & Arrow Link Placement:** TropicalAEC™ maintains an authoritative architectural whitepaper aesthetic. Never use emojis (no pointing fingers, thumbs, or decorative symbols in text or links). For callouts and cross-references, always place a clean typographical arrow *before* the link pointing directly at it (e.g., `&rarr; [Guide Title](url)`), never trailing after the link text.
>
> **Strictly No Numbered Subtitles in Features or Reports:** Features and Reports must use clean descriptive architectural titles (e.g., `## The Marine Chloride Penetration Zone`, `## Cyclonic Uplift Engineering`). Do not prefix headings with arbitrary numbers (never use `## 1.`, `## 2.`). Numbering is strictly reserved for Listicles (numbered comparison items) and Tutorials (sequential execution steps).

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative breakdowns of tropical envelope challenges (e.g., salt-spray fastener failure, island solar microgrids, Hawaiian single-wall retrofits).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Styled callout container with executive summary text followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately (e.g., *Two miles from the surf. Ocean breezes carrying microscopic salt aerosol through open windows. Beneath the paint on your roof brackets, chloride ions are quietly eating through galvanized steel...*).
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never write dense multi-sentence blocks.

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of cyclonic wind codes, battery storage economics at $0.45/kWh, and marine concrete restoration.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Comparison Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Data Teardown:** Physical measurements (basic wind speed in MPH, salt-fog hours to failure under ASTM B117, levelized cost of energy [LCOE] per kWh, tensile strength in PSI).
  4. **Code Compliance Box:** Exact ASCE 7-22, Hawaii State Building Code (HRS Chapter 107), or Puerto Rico Building Code citations.

### Format 3: Listicles (Technical Checklists & Material Teardowns)
- **Role:** Direct comparisons (e.g., *5 Marine-Grade Fasteners That Will Not Rust Within 1 Mile of the Beach*, *4 Impact-Rated Architectural Louver Systems*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Context $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Technical Subheadings $\rightarrow$ Buyer/Builder Verdict.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Metadata.**
  2. **Numbered H2 Headings:** `## 1. Material / Component Name` (Numbers permitted exclusively in Listicles).
  3. **Each item must include:** Technical metric table (Cost per unit, Metallurgy grade, Salt-spray rating, Uplift design pressure).

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Process Guides)
- **Role:** Actionable, code-compliant execution workflows (e.g., *How to Repair Spalling Rebar in Concrete Slabs*, *Installing Marine-Grade 316 Hurricane Clips*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Overview $\rightarrow$ Code/Permit Requisites $\rightarrow$ Sequential Execution Steps $\rightarrow$ Inspection Checklist.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Quick Summary.**
  2. **Jobsite Requisites Table:** Tools, PPE, anti-corrosive primers, concrete chipping hammers.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** `## Step 1: Delamination Sounding & Concrete Chipping`.
  4. **Pro-Tip & Safety Warning Callouts:** Emphasizing eye protection from chipping concrete, rebar structural depth limits, and high-heat hydration curing control in tropical sun.

## 3. The "Empower First, Safety Net Second" Protocol

Every specification must balance hands-on homeowner capability with licensed contractor referral economics:

1. **Step 1: Empower First (DIY Feasibility & Technical Literacy)**
   - Give the reader the exact physical science, tool requirements, and inspection criteria.
   - Demystify the process so they understand exactly what needs to be done.
2. **Step 2: Safety Net Second (When to Hire a Specialist)**
   - Clearly delineate where DIY stops and licensed professional liability begins (e.g., repairing structural post-and-pier foundation rot, engineering 180 MPH roof tie-downs, installing 48V / 240V high-capacity lithium battery systems).
   - Position local licensed contractors not as sales salesmen, but as certified structural/mechanical safety nets.
3. **Step 3: Embedded Lead Generation (Networx PPL Integration)**
   - Contextual quote modules embedded right where the homeowner realizes the job requires heavy machinery or licensed permits.
